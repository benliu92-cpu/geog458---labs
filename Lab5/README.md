# Study Area and dates:
For this project, I selected the Lower New York harbor area, focuing on the waters near Sandy Hook and the entrance to New York Harbor. I chose this region because it is an importnat shipping corridor where passenger, cargo, and tanker vessels travel in and out of the harbor. The dataset covers December 24-31, 2025. The data came from AccessAIS, which provides historical vessel tracking data. To keep the dataset manageble, I selected a smaller geographic area and limited the time range to just one week. AccessAIS timestamps are UTC, and my map uses New York local time.
# Interpretation:
Looking at my map, I noticed differences in the vessel paths when switching between different vessel types. For example, passenger vessels seems to be more active during day time. While tanker and and cargo all follow the same routes. Showing a almost straight line on the map. They also have a more random timing traveling on the water compare the passengers. These patterns suggest that different vessel categories use the harbor for different purposes. The animation and time slider allow me to examine how vessel positions change throuoghout the selected period. 
# Feature proposal for a potential user:
A potential user of my visualization would be a harbor traffic manager responsible for onitoring vessel movemments and identifying potentially congested waterways. Base on the resource, I would propose adding a vessel traffic density heatmap to my visualization. This feature would highlight areas where vessels frequently travel. User could select a vessel type and time to view areas with high traffic activity.
